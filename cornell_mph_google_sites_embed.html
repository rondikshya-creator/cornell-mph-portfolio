About me · Leadership narrative

Public health rooted in people and place

My path to public health began with a foundation in biology and global health at Rollins College and has grown through community-based research, occupational health, environmental science, and program evaluation. I am now pursuing a Master of Public Health at Cornell University, concentrating in Environment, Climate, and Health.

Across my experiences, I have seen that health is shaped not only by individual choices, but also by working conditions, access to safe water and food, environmental exposures, and opportunities to be heard. My work has included household water treatment education in the Dominican Republic, occupational health resources for workers in Nepal, soil health research in New York, and community engagement at Cornell.

These experiences have helped me approach public health leadership as a practice of listening, collaborating, and translating evidence into useful action. I continue to develop skills in environmental assessment, health communication, GIS, and evaluation design. As I move toward graduation, I hope to contribute to public health efforts that make healthier environments and systems more accessible and equitable.

Applied Practice Experience · Cashmere House of Nepal

Occupational health, worker wellbeing & sustainability

Kathmandu, Nepal · Bilingual English–Nepali resources

This APEx project developed practical resources to support worker safety, respectful workplaces, and environmentally responsible practices in a cashmere production setting. Both deliverables translate occupational and environmental health concepts into tools for workplace education and assessment.

Deliverable 1 · Work Safe. Stay Well.

A one-page educational safety sheet for factory and weaving workers covering clear walkways, safe tool use, physical comfort, hydration, respect, and early hazard reporting. The bilingual format is intended to support accessible workplace communication.

Deliverable 2 · Workplace assessment resource

A bilingual resource with structured checklists, worker discussion prompts, risk documentation, and an improvement-planning template. It addresses safety, wellbeing, inclusion, resource conservation, and waste practices. Its assessment fields are templates to be completed with workplace observations and worker input, not reported findings.

Coursework · VTPEH 6175

GIS Suitability Analysis

Tompkins County, New York · Fall 2026

Created a QGIS map identifying four parcels for further investigation as potential manufactured home community sites. The analysis considered parcel size, designated opportunity zones, proximity to healthcare, and mapped flood zones. The map identifies candidates for further investigation, not approved development sites.
